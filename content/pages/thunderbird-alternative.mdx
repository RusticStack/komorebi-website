Thunderbird and Komorebi are both free and open source. Thunderbird does far more. Komorebi does less, on purpose, in a smaller Windows-only app.

## At a glance

<Compare>

| | Komorebi | Thunderbird |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Free | Free |
| Licence | GPL-3.0-or-later | MPL 2.0 |
| Systems | Windows 10 and 11 | Windows, macOS, Linux |
| Account types | IMAP and SMTP | IMAP, POP3, SMTP, plus news and feeds |
| Sign-in | Password or app password | Includes OAuth (Google, Microsoft) |
| Calendar and contacts | Yes, CalDAV and CardDAV | Yes, CalDAV and CardDAV |
| OpenPGP and S/MIME | No | Yes, built in |
| Add-ons | No | Yes |
| HTML mail engine | Its own layout engine, no scripts | Gecko, Mozilla's web engine |
| Telemetry | None | Sent to Mozilla; you can turn it off |

</Compare>

## When Komorebi fits

- You want a small app that looks like Windows 11.
- You want no telemetry at all, with nothing to turn off.
- IMAP and SMTP with a password cover your accounts.

## When Thunderbird fits better

- You need OpenPGP or S/MIME.
- You use Gmail or Microsoft accounts that require OAuth.
- You need macOS or Linux, POP3, or add-ons.
